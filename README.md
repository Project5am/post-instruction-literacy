Legibility of Formation

Post Instruction Literacy begins with a loss.

Not a loss of truth, certainty, or information. A loss of visibility into how something became what it is.

Decisions, institutions, categories, explanations, artworks, and beliefs often arrive as settled objects. Their formation disappears behind their appearance. The conditions that shaped them, the pressures that constrained them, the alternatives that were available, and the processes through which they emerged become difficult to see. What remains is the outcome, often presented as inevitable, self-evident, or complete.

Post Instruction Literacy is a response to this loss. Its centre of gravity is legibility of formation, the capacity to perceive how something came to be what it is. This concern operates in two directions. Formation can be read prospectively while a field remains live and unsettled. Formation can also be read retrospectively through traces preserved in texts, decisions, institutions, categories, and other settled outcomes. The question in both cases is the same: what remains visible of the process that produced the result?

Threshold names the point at which formation remains directly observable. Low Closure Pressure describes the capacity to remain at threshold without forcing settlement before available signal warrants it. Post Instruction Literacy is the literacy that develops under these conditions. It concerns the reading of formation, the detection of closure pressures, and the preservation of visibility where compression, categorisation, and premature settlement would otherwise conceal it.

PIL does not claim universal access to formation. Some objects preserve rich traces of their becoming. Others retain very little. Where formation remains legible, PIL can read. Where formation has been concealed, erased, or compressed beyond recovery, PIL encounters its limits.

Examples

A practice presents a body of work. Legibility of formation concerns the peak experiences, failed attempts, discarded directions, ancestral inheritances, and structural encounters through which that work became possible.

A framework presents a vocabulary. Legibility of formation concerns the origin events, unplanned conflicts, institutional resistances, and accumulated recognitions through which those terms became necessary.

A release presents a finished sound. Legibility of formation concerns the influences absorbed, the experiments abandoned, the conditions of production, and the decisions about what to carry forward and what to leave behind.

An identity presents a position. Legibility of formation concerns lineage, encounters with gatekeeping, moments of reorientation, and the gradual process of naming what was already operating.

A deposit presents itself as complete. Legibility of formation concerns the open field from which the deposit emerged and the conditions that allowed its recognition.

In each case, the settled outcome remains visible. Post Instruction Literacy concerns the visibility of its formation.
